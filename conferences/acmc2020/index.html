ACMC 2020

The Australasian Computer Music Conference 2020 was hosted by the Australian National University, Canberra, and held online from 3 to 11 July 2020 because of the COVID-19 pandemic, with no registration fee. Its theme was inclusion.

Conference website: https://australasian-computer-music-association.github.io/acmc2020/

Announcement (December 2019)

We’re excited to announce that the Australasian Computer Music Conference will take place at the Australian National University, Canberra, from July 8-11, 2020.

Website and Proceedings: https://australasian-computer-music-association.github.io/acmc2020/

ACMC is the annual gathering of the Australasian Computer Music Association, a place to show and discuss research and artistic progress in computer/electronic music. It’s a festival of performances, installations, workshops, and tutorials that inspire, challenge, and showcase our community.

This year’s conference theme is inclusion. We want to highlight the diverse range of people who contribute to Australia’s computer/electronic music culture but may not normally attend an academic conference, including artists and scholars who are young/emerging, from regional/remote areas, who have a disability, who are First Nations People, who have low/no income, who are engaged in community music making, or who simply work outside of academia and mainstream institutions.

As part of these efforts, we have an early call for contributions open from 17 December 2019 with the opportunity to provide feedback and engage in conversation with all interested participants. Further calls will be published in 2020.

Early contributions or questions can be directed to conference@computermusic.org.au or addressed through the form on the conference website.

Looking forward to your submissions!

Proceedings

ACMC 2020: Proceedings of the 2020 Australasian Computer Music Conference. Australasian Computer Music Association, July 2020. There is no printed volume: the proceedings are the conference website, which published each extended abstract and program note with its session recording. Each keynote, abstract, workshop and creative work below has its own page with a citation, its abstract or program note, the presenters’ biographies and links to the recordings. Full versions of accepted papers were invited to Chroma.

Review process and committees

Papers, artist talks, and creative works will selected through a single-blind peer-review process with at least two reviewers. Workshops and tutorials will be selected by the conference organisers. […] All extended abstracts will be subject to single-blind review by at least two peer reviewers.
