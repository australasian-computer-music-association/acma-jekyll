The name of the piece refers to the four provinces (or cúigí) of Ireland. The native folk music of Ireland, much like its dialectical language, has distinct territorial styles, that not only informs the ornamentation and instrumental technique of its practitioners but also the repertoire. Over the course of time, the exact boundaries of stylistic distinctions between the regions becomes blurred through musical interaction and cross pollination.

Using samples of instrumental music, Irish language radio broadcasts and environmental field recordings from key regions, this real world weaving of sound and music is mimicked in a computer composition through digital synthesis and processing. In the first part of the piece a fiddle performance of a slow “air” or lament is decimated beyond recognition by feeding it through a Max/MSP patch that captures input and triggers short buffers at playback speeds 1/10th of the original. In the second half more fiddle performances are deconstructed using a 4 channel granular synthesis environment also built in Max/MSP
