One day the wind blew across the open bathroom window as though it were playing harmonics on a giant flute. Ayers recorded the sounds, which included amazing glissandi and harmonics, a “staccato whistle” and a “Chinese opera soprano,” which was especially expressive filtered and fluttered. Amplified rain clicks and a car door slam produced percussive effects. The singing, whistling and general moaning of the wind reminded her of ghost voices, and inspired her to contrast those sounds with the ghosts of real composers’ voices in her composition, Ghost Winds Talking.
