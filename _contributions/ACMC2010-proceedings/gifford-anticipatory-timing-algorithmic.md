Generative music algorithms frequently operate by making musical decisions in a sequence, with each step of the sequence incorporating the local musical context in the decision process. Non-real-time algorithms often incorporate both past and future events in the context. Real-time algorithms, in contrast, generally include only past events since the future is unknown. However, even real-time agents can incorporate knowledge of their own future actions by utilising some form of planning. We outline a process for real-time rhythmic generation that incorporates a limited form of planning we call anticipatory timing – that offers a worthwhile trade-off between musical coherence and efficiency. We give an example of a real-time generative agent - the Jambot that utilises anticipatory timing for rhythmic generation. We describe its operation, and compare its output with and without anticipatory timing.
