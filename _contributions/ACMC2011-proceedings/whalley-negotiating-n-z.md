The contention is that it is worthwhile to combine some mainstream historical genres found in New Zealand sonic art – electroacoustic music, traditional Māori instruments, found/invented instruments, and Asian influenced approaches; and that newer production techniques provide a means of negotiating between different sonic art perspectives in both real-time and netspace. Some early experiments by the author toward this are outlined, particularly in combining tradition Māori instruments and electroacoustic music. The work Mittsu no Yugo (Whalley 2010), a telemusic event between Beijing, Hamilton, and Calgary for MUSICACOUSTICA10, is then discussed as a recent working example of the proposition.
