This study considers two recordings of I am Sitting in a Room in terms of their sound. In physical terms, the spectral content, transfer function between generations, and apparent room reverberation time are examined. Several psychoacoustical algorithms are applied to the sound recordings, in order to rate their sensory qualities. The experimental part of the study involves subjective assessments of a portion of each generation of the two recordings. Three evaluation scales and two emotion scales were used. Relationships between psychoacoustical and subjective measurements are examined. Together, the three parts of the study indicate that the 1980 recording is more appealing and more subtle than the 1970 recording.
