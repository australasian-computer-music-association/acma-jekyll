Since the 1960s New Zealand composers have shown an interest in working native birdsong into their music. This paper examines the use that various composers – most notably Douglas Lilburn – have made of tape recordings of North Island kokako made by John Kendrick of the Wildlife Division of the Department of Internal Affairs (c.1969). In addition, the paper includes a presentation of a new electroacoustic work of the author, entitled Kokako Triptych – a work composed with a full knowledge of, and making deliberate reference to, these earlier compositions.
