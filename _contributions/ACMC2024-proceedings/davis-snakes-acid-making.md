Music generation using Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools has become more prevalent over recent years with websites such as SUNO.com allowing anyone to create of music using prompts within an easy-to-use web-based environment. This paper sets out to document the author’s ongoing experiments using (AI) tools to create acid house music. This paper sets out to evaluate the suitability of Facebook MusicGen within the Python environment to create acid house music. This study uses an autoethnographic approach to examine the author’s music making practices over a 12-month period. This paper discusses methods, outcomes and problems of using Python and MusicGen and offers potential uses for artists to develop their own music using these tools. This article suggests that command line interface (CLI) for tools such as Python are not particularly user friendly for musicians from a non-coding background. Issues identified in this paper are difficult (CLI) user interface, the lack of clear documentation, errors within current documentation, issues with operating systems and environments, and problems with existing libraries and tools. This paper also documents the limitations within the AI’s training material. Facebook MusicGen is not trained acid house music as a sub-genre of electronic dance music and as such prompt-based approaches did not bring expected results. The paper suggest that MusicGen can be viewed as a useful studio collaborator, that gives a starting point rather than a finished product. Whilst the paper documents issues and shortcomings with these AI tools, there were some positive outcomes. The tools were able to produce audio material that could be used for inspiration, beginning points, interesting rhythms, background or additional material, such as textures, pads and drones. This paper posits that with increased training in specific sub genres it maybe be possible to get music outputs that better represent the acid house genre.
