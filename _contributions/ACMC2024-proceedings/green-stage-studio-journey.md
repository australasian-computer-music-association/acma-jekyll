This presentation explores the artist’s transitional journey from being a live performing pianist, to a music composer, arranger, and producer using a digital audio workstation. Using Cubase as her primary DAW, she discusses producing tracks that feature the piano as a melodic, harmonic and/or rhythmic instrument. The ways in which music technology, specifically using a DAW alongside plug-ins and virtual instruments, has allowed her to re-imagine the sound and role of the piano in music performance and production will be highlighted. The presentation also examines the artist’s varying emotional responses towards her music-making since she embraced music technology. A variety of musical examples will be provided to contextualise the discussion. The pieces cover a variety of tones and textures alongside genres, often fusing orchestral and electronic sounds. The presentation outlines compositional and arrangement techniques specific to the pieces being discussed and offers an overview of the plug-ins and virtual instruments used to achieve the desired sound. These include Spitfire Audio’s LABS and BBC Symphony Orchestra, and Output’s pulse engine, Signal. The artist will also address the financial considerations involved in selecting virtual instruments. The presentation includes a brief overview of the field, situating the artist's practice in relation to current practices and trends. It also covers other aspects of her professional journey, including her shift from composing via score writing programs to using a DAW. The artist shares her motivations for embracing music technology which include entering the field of screen composition and producing music for library music companies.
