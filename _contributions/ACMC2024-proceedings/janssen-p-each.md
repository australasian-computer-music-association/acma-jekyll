(p)each, inspired by the popular Japanese folktale Momotaro, responds to, and is also inspired by grassroots movements across the world and troubled times. The story of a boy born from a peach, who gathers unlikely allies, to fight demonic oppressors offers an example of bravery against unmatched odds, of how when we work together we can do the seemingly impossible. It is a story of people’s struggles to overcome oppression, a fight for equality and justice, across time and cultures. In 2019 berni encountered Momotaro on a visit to Okayama, birthplace of the legend. The story stuck and struck a chord when weeks later, and in Hong Kong, where over a million people gathered to protest, one of the first of many many demonstrations. The streets were filled with people and energy and hope. There was a death at that demonstration, a young man in a yellow raincoat, fell/jumped/was pushed. This is the seed that grew into (p)each. Vicki and berni had their first creative meeting around the work in October 2019. Then the COVID times where they continued to work, remotely, allowing them the time to enrich the work through further research and to create a shared language. The ongoing worldwide injustices and people’s fight for an equitable world continued to add layers to the original story. berni’s texts drawn from these stories, protest chants, manga sound effects are performed live and as pre-records, creating sonic and poetic texture. Entwining with the live and pre-recorded vocal texts and effects, the treated clarinet creates emotional and evocative layers with improvised thematic, timbral and textural material. Using the clarinet as another “voice”, Hallett comments, responds, emotes the struggles and fights while also recognising the spirit and bravery of those fighting the oppressors. berni m janssen is a maker of poems, performances, multidisciplinary art and preserves. She often works with composers, musicians, sound artists, performers and visual artists. She lives and works on the unceded land of the Dja Dja Wurrung in Australia. Vicki Hallett is a musician, acoustic ecologist and composer. Her focus is to record, compose with, and feature ever-diminishing habitats and species. Highlighting unique sounds from the micro to the macro, Hallett utilises sounds we rarely or cannot hear. Hallett travels the world recording sounds as well as performing in environments in Africa (with hippopotami) and the Amazon jungle. www.vickihallett.com
