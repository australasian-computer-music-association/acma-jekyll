The imagination is considered one of, if not the most powerful tools that define us as human beings. Through this most illustrious gift, we have had the ability to first dream up and then bring into reality ideas and conceptions that have been crucial in the evolution and development of mankind, through technological, social, and cultural advancement. This research investigates how the imagination can be intentionally influenced by external sources; explicitly - visual imagery, sound environments, and suggestive stimuli of the natural world, and how they can be used as a creative device in the act of creativity in the musical arts. This study compares what is already established in the fields of research associated with human influence - including the origins of western inspiration; the power of suggestion; directed imagination; colours and meaning; visual imagery in music; the subliminal effects of film music; the emotional influence of colour and sound; and the perceptions of the sonic environment, to assess how and if directing the imagination of a musical artist can help drive further creativity during live performance. The research conducted includes an experiment that investigates to what extent a creative artist may be influenced under the guidance of a directed imagination task, that involves participants improvising music on their chosen instrument to a series of visual, audible, and suggestive stimuli related to nature. Three landscape settings of the natural world are used as the basis of the suggestive stimuli – Rainforest, Ocean Shore, and Thunderstorm, with the intention to invoke diverse influence and emotional implication on each participant, to help answer the question; How do Visual Imagery, Nature Soundscapes, and Thematic Concepts help the improvising musician tell a story during performance?
