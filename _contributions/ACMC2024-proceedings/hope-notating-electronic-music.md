Notation and electronic music do not have a long or stable history together. For some, this is part of the appeal of making electronic music – it is immediate, open and unshackled from the traditions of acoustic music systems that notation was designed to serve. However, music notation has played an important role in the remembering, and passing on for the performance of, music ideas. Whilst electronic music has recording as an efficient way to remember –it is not a very efficient tool to communicate information for those who may wish to recreate it in performance. Despite numerous artefacts of the creation of electronic music, such as recordings, software programs and wave form images, we are still not able to recreate live electronic music performance effectively from these. This keynote explores the potential of notation for electronic music, and argues that it provides rich protentional for bringing musicians of different music styles together to render composers ideas. Notation can be rethought and designed so that it can provide a useful and relevant tool that enables collaborative music making for electronic artists, and facilitate a broader range of music that reflects today’s music practices for performers now and into the future.
