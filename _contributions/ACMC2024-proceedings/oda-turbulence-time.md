The Universe is not limited to spatial one, but might be temporal one, namely temporal diversity. I used two morphed tracks, and the one is the morphing between Kyrie from J.S.Bach’ Mass in B minor (of which copyright has expired) and my own sound system which treats sounds as many particles according to turbulence algorithm using Unity, a game engine, and the other is the morphing between Dona nobis pacem from Mass and my live system which is using AI algorithm. Therefore, there are two turbulences, namely, the one is the turbulence of past and the other is that of future. We experience these two turbulences and become the turbulence of present in ourselves. There have been many attempts that treat past or ancient music as elements for current composition, but this time, I tried to construct a huge temporal object which vibrates between the past and the current techniques of composing, to head toward a future composing. In visual, against Baudrillard' concept "Hyperreality", computational physical simulation does not have to obey the mere imitation of real world. We can use computational simulation as a material for expressing something artistic. Actually, I used turbulence algorithm in order to make sound materials emit as mere objects in vast space, and also used oceanic simulation for making very odd 3D objects in this piece. Independent to actual reality, simulation has not been that of reality, but became simulation itself – this can mean that we could use simulation for artistic expression freely, as this piece.
