Doom’s First score became "one of the first truly iconic soundtracks in modern gaming” while the music of the later iterations of the same franchise came to “(define) the sound of DOOM”. This sound is predicated on a series of complex aesthetic and conceptual remixes (Waysdorf) that expand upon the aesthetic of “heavy metal” and narratives of sci-fi- horror. Gordon Remixes elements of Prince’s original sound, music but also sound effects, that is, “…the use of preexisting material to make something new”. To remix is to compose…an act that question(s) authorship, creativity, originality, and the economics that supported the discourse behind these terms as stable cultural forms (Navas). Mick Gordon’s use of Prince’s compositions (themselves remixes) is firstly a Reflexive remix; where the remixed music challenges the “spectacular aura” (Navas) of the original music and narrative. This remix is further than the aesthetic form (Waysdorf), challenging the original narrative with his new settings, Gordon offers a communicative remix (Waysdorf) of the world of Doom. In the presentation form of this analysis, Prince and Gordon’s collective work on a series of remixes titled, At Doom’s Gate, will be discussed.
