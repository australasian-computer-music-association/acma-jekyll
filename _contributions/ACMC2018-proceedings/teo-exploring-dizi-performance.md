In musical performance, three questions are often asked: what is the contribution of the instrument? What is the contribution of the player? Which musical exercise was performed? Here, we attempt to objectively quantify and compare the effects of the player, instrument and performed exercise by analyzing audio samples collected from a series of musical exercises performed on the traditional and modernized Chinese transverse flute, Dízi, for 5 expert players. Feature extraction is employed on the samples and a machine learning classifier algorithm is applied to the same dataset to confidently identify distinct populations: firstly, separating traditional vs modernized Dízi, secondly, separating player identity, and finally identifying the musical exercise performed all based purely on the Dízi performance’s acoustic output alone.
