bio: Brona Martin is an electroacoustic composer and sound artist from Banagher, Co. Offaly, Ireland. Following a BA in History and Geography at the University of Dublin, Brona studied music at the University of Cork where she had her first encounter with computer music, noise and experimental music. At the University of Limerick she completed a Masters in Music Technology with Jürgen Simpson and Dr. Kerry Hagan. After some teaching and traveling Brona started her PhD in September 2010 under the supervision of Dr. David Berezan. Research interests include soundscape composition, augmented auralities, acoustic ecology and narrative within Electroacoustic music.

This piece investigates the illusion of the internal and external sound world of a church/cathedral space and the sound- world within those sounds. The church building is treated as a deconstructed sound object exposing the multiple layers within. Space is explored both as intimate/internal and as distant. “The most salient sound signal in the Christian community is the church bell”. (R.M Schafer). This sound can dominate a busy city landscape such as Oxford Road, in Manchester where even peak time traffic and the volume created by 40,000 students cannot drown it out. Therefore the church bell introduces the listener into this new electroacoustic soundscape before focusing on the micro sounds within this environment. These include the smallest movements within the church such as the movement of a person against the wood, people murmuring their prayers and lighting candles and how they are amplified by this space. The aim of the piece is to create an illusion that connects the listener to an environment that one is perhaps very familiar with. However it is created to make the listener aware of the acoustic properties contained within this large reverberant space that has dominated our landscape for centuries.
