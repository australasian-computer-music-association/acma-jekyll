This study investigates qualities of human movement that relate to the artistic organisation of sound. Identification of human movement qualities can assist in making expressive computer music interfaces and sound art installations which respond appropriately to expressive human movement.

In this paper, we present an empirical approach to exploring the relationship between sound and movement, in which participants are asked to move in response to sounds selected randomly from a palette of samples based on a mapping between Laban movement qualities and spectromorphological properties. Participants’ movement is recorded using a depth-sensing computer vision system and later analysed with respect to the movement qualities exhibited. Participants are also asked to verbalise their thoughts and responses in a follow up interview.

The collected data, will be used in the design of an interactive installation based on the principles of the relationships between sound, movement and cognition established through our investigation.
