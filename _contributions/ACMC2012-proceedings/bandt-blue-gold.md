bio: Ros Bandt is an award winning international sound artist from Australia who is famous for her soundings and installations of unusual acoustic spaces from wheat silos, water tanks, limestonequarries, swimming pools, ancient deserts, historic buildings, and underwater environments. She has pioneered the use of endangered sounds in her sound installations and radiophonic works contributing to environmental and sound culture awareness, recording and interpreting Indigenous and multi-cultural languages in change, native wild life, old machinery and lost classical languages. She has built soundplaygrounds, interactive systems, global satellite performances, made sculptures, musical instruments and is an expert sound recordist and musician. She has interpreted sites and sound cultures worldwide including the Goatherd songs of Crete, the endangered sound of the Isobue, Japanese Sea Whistle, and the fleeting sounds of her own Aeolian harps and casuarinas in Lake Mungo. While living in Europe
  in 2010-11 she toured her international collaborative sound art performance with hair dye paintings with Johannes S Sistermanns Germany, Tracings CD Wergo, and composing and performing on Tarhu the Black Flacon CD with Erdem Helvacioglu, Pozitif Yappim, CD of the week in Istanbul. Her site specific electro-acoustic symphony, Medusa Dreaming was composed for the Yerebatan Sarayi underwater cistern in Istanbul, performing two concerts there with the Medusa ensemble she created for this event. She is a founding member of six ensembles incuding the early music ensemble La Romanesca with whom she is currently touring.

Blue gold is an electroacoustic work for cello and clarinets, sculptures and digital colour field and moving imagery. It investigates the delicate balance between wet and dry, hugros, xeros, symbolised in the colours of the title BLUE GOLD. The performers, cello (wet) and the clarinets (dry), navigate their way through the electroacoustic sound fields sourced from the Southern Ocean underwater recordings, aeolian harps in the arid Mungo desert, flash storms in the bush and the wetlands of the Otway Ranges. A range of ecstatic dances composed in the Peloponnese, Greece, remind us that European settlement has reformed and changed the Australian landscape irrevocably. The performers respond to soundscape recordings, the imagery, the graphic and notated scores, to bring this alive in each place they perform, inside or outside, desert or flood. They pour earth and water into the glass timer sculptures or the large bamboo beam balance showing the changing balances between the water and the earth, and the role we are playing in its care and destruction. These resonate at the metaphorical levels of civilization/nature, and european/indigenous perspectives. Water, land care and global warming are surely the greatest challenges facing us and demand attentiveness. BLUE GOLD is a plea to become more mindful and not take so much.

BLUE GOLD was commissioned, premiered and toured by charisma, ros dunlop, clarinets and julia ryder, cello. thanks to canadian activist maude barlow for her encouragement and permission to use the title inspired by her book blue gold: the fight to stop the corporate theft of the world’s water, 2003.
