This paper looks at three animated scores based on the work of American conceptual artist Sol LeWitt (1928–2007): Ryan Ross Smith’s “Study no. 38 [Variations on Sol LeWitt’s Variations of Incomplete Open Cubes]” (2014), Cat Hope’s “Wall Drawing” for string quartet, theremin and electronics (2014) and Justin Yang’s “LeWitt Notations I” for improviser, live-generated animation/score and live electronics (2010). This paper provides a technical and artistic analysis of these works, and how each composer’s respective approach to Animated Notation relates to LeWitt’s work as well as his seminal 1967 paper, “Paragraphs on Conceptual Art”.
