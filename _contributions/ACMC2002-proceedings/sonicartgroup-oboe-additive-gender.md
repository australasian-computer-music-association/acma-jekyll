bio: The Sonic Art Group is based in the School of Music – Conservatorium at Monash University. Its major role is to function as a research unit directed by Peter Mcilwain – Lecturer in Composition. In addition, it provides opportunities for composition students at the School to gain experience in experimental computer based music. Since starting at the beginning of 2001 it has; performed in a combined concert with Simulation (a group based at QUT) and at the Make It Up Club and has staged the Electronic Antiphony concert. The group’s creative work is focused on the composition of sonic textures through improvisation. Much of this work is centred around software developed by the Peter Mcilwain, in conjunction with other members of the group.

The gendèr is a metallophone found in the Java gamelan orchestra. It is pronounced gendèr (-g) with a “g” as in “get” and with the “er” syllable pronounced “air”. The Additive Gendèr is a software instrument - its name referring to the synthesis method used and the characteristic sounds that it produces. The performance of this work features Andrew Ross on oboe and Peter Mcilwain on Additive Gendèr.
