bio: |-
  Born in Melbourne Australia, Paul Doornbusch is a composer, sonologist, and occasional performer who works largely with algorithmic composition systems for traditional instruments and electronics. His work is presented internationally in concerts throughout Europe (Paris, Amsterdam, The Hague, Frankfurt, Köln, Berlin, Salzburg, etc.) and also in Australasia, Canada and the USA. His recent works include Continuity 3 for percussion and computer and Continuity 2 for electronics and bass recorders (premiered Salzburg festival 1999). Doornbusch's music is concerned with rich textures and elaborate but transparent musical structures that probe the extremes at every level for the performer and listener. Currently working at RMITs Interactive Information Institute, Doornbusch is involved with music and sound in technological and Virtual Reality contexts.

  Timothy Phillips is one of Australia's most versatile young percussionists, having forged a career both here and in Germany playing in orchestras, new music ensembles and as a soloist and chamber musician. He has appeared at the London Proms, Edinburgh Festival, Salzburg Festspiele with groups such as Ensemble Modern, the Ensemble Modern Orchestra, KLangforum Vienna and World Brass. Currently based in Melbourne he performs with the Melbourne Symphony, Libra Ensemble and Astra Chamber Music Society.

Continuity 3 has as its unifying theme concerns of fragmentation and continuity. Many of our experiences may be continuous, for example time, light and so on, but they may in fact be fragmented such as memories, or described as particles such as light. This duality and tension of opposed structures is explored in Continuity 3. The instrumentation produces both musically "pure" and fragmented sounds, from the plate and chinese cymbal respectively. The electronics is entirely processing of these sounds, and either makes them more continuous or fragmented. For example, resynthesised overtones from an instrument might hold for a long time or they might rapidly change pitch and timbre. The overall structure starts with continuous sounds quickly fragmenting and slowly coming to a continuous and unified section. This becomes excited and fragmented by the electronics and eventually bursts into a frenzy of activity. The process continues with variations before the ending which presents these opposing elements again.
