"The Mossy Slopes of Mt. Meru" (2002) is a piece made with scales developed from additive number series that are found by adding up the diagonals of Pingala's Meru Prastara, known in the West as Pascal's Triangle. Following suggestions by Ervin Wilson, I developed a family of 30 scales from the first 5 number series, and play them, for this performance, with sine waves. These sine waves carve up the space they are played in into various spatial configurations which can easily be heard by moving one's head slightly as one listens. Thus, tuning, timbre, and sound-space architecture become unified into a single musical gesture. I certainly don’t expect the listener to be interested in following, or able to follow all the arithmetical things I did to make this piece. But I made the piece to find SOUNDS, and it is these sound that I now wish to share with whoever is interested in experiencing them.
