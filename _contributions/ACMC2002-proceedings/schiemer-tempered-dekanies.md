bio: Greg Schiemer (b.1949) is a composer and instrument-builder who has been active in the Sydney experimental music scene since 1970. His dance-theatre work Brolga (1973) was one of the earliest developments in Australian computer music. In collaboration with experimental choreographer Phillippa Cullen (1972-75) he created interactive electronic music using theremins as a control interface for dance movement. His Spectral Dance (1992), Token Objects (1993) and Shantivanam (1996), performed at festivals in Australasia, Greece, Japan & Hong Kong, involve the use his purpose-built interactive electronic instrument designs such as the Tupperware Gamelan and the MIDI Tool Box. He has been active in the development of more recent new music technology as recipient of an Australia Council Composer’s Fellowship at the CSIRO Division of Radiophysics (1994) and as chief investigator in a Collaborative Australian Research Council Grant with Lake Technology as industry partner (1997). His just intonation
  studies Transposed Hexanies, were presented at ACMA, QUT (2000) and at MicroFest, Los Angeles (2001). He lectures in electronic music at Sydney Conservatorium of Music where he is currently the acting chair of composition.

Combination product sets - just intonation scales created by contemporary tuning theorist Erv Wilson – are formed when combinations of a set of harmonics are multiplied together. The dekany is a 10-note CPS scale generated by multiplying all combinations of pairs of harmonics from a set of five harmonics. Tempered Dekanies uses two dekanies; the first generated from harmonics 1, 3, 7, 9 and 11, the second from harmonics 1, 3, 5, 7 and 9.

The title takes its name from the pitch deviation that is a central feature of the piece. The deviation is an extension of Risset’s chorusing instrument in which each pitch is heard together with a collection of microtonally detuned pitches, tuned above and below a central pitch. Unlike Risset’s chorus where the bend above and below the central pitch is equal, the effect achieved here is produced using by a pair of just and non-equal microtonal ratios, 100/99 above and 99/98 below. Detuning takes place gradually over the duration of each note to produce beats that gradually accelerate and decelerate throughout the piece. The work is realised using Csound.
