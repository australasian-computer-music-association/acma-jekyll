## **CompMusic '93**

10 July 1993, Wesley College, University of Sydney, Sydney, Australia

CompMusic '93 was a one-day symposium with two evening concerts in the Old Darlington School. It was the first in what became the annual ACMA conference series. The convener was Gordon Monro, with Fiona Allen and Anthony Hood. The day included a panel discussion, "Where is the music in computer music?", with David Hirst (ACMA President), Ian Fredericks, Peter McCallum, David Worrall, Linda Ceff and Stephen Adam, and a demonstration by Roland Australia.

### **Proceedings**

No proceedings volume is known. ACMA planned to publish the papers as the first issue of a new journal, *Mikropolyphonie*, "to accommodate the entire proceedings of the CompMusic '93 Symposium" ([Chroma 14]({% link chroma_pdf/Chroma14.pdf %})). We have not found a copy. If you have one, please [get in touch](mailto:info@computermusic.org.au).

### **Papers**

No programme survives. Peter McCallum's review in the *Sydney Morning Herald* (reprinted in [Chroma 14]({% link chroma_pdf/Chroma14.pdf %}), pp. 10–11) mentions papers by:

- Gordon Monro, and Jeff Pressing and Chris Scallan, on lagged embedding
- Tim Kreger, on modifying melodies with RNA/DNA relationships from *Gödel, Escher, Bach*
- Richard Orton, on *Tabula Vigilans*
- Thomas Stainsby, David Hirst and Nigel Nettheim, on music analysis
- Robert Bell and Linda Ceff, on instrumental timbre
- Jeff Pressing, on *Transcribe*, a program for automatic notation
- Brian Henderson-Sellers, on MAX
- Greg Schiemer, on local area networks for interactive improvisation
