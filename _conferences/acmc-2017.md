## **Do Androids Dream of Computer Music?**

Elder Conservatorium of Music, The University of Adelaide, 28 September – 1 October 2017

The Australasian Computer Music Association invites composers, performers, academics, engineers and technologists to present research and creative works exploring interdisciplinary perspectives on music making, with a special focus on emerging technologies, automation and robotics in music. This conference aims to expand our current understandings of automated and semi-autonomous music making and its impact in the broader cultural landscape.

## Proceedings

*Do Androids Dream of Computer Music? Proceedings of the Australasian Computer Music Conference 2017*, edited by Luke Harrald and Barnabas Smith. Australasian Computer Music Association, September 2017. ISSN 1448-7780.
Each paper, abstract, performance work and artist talk below has its own page with a citation.

[Complete proceedings (PDF)]({% link proceedings/ACMC2017-proceedings.pdf %}) · [Conference programme (PDF)]({% link proceedings/ACMC2017-proceedings/ACMC-2017-program.pdf %})<!-- the programme is the ACMA website's own copy (media/2017/10/ACMC-2017-program.pdf), recovered from a 2022 backup of the site; the conference website's copies were never archived -->

The performance works were played in two concerts in Elder Hall: Concert 1 on Friday 29 September at 1 pm, and Concert 2 on Saturday 30 September at 6:30 pm (announced on the conference website as "The Music of Tomorrow"). Each work's concert comes from the conference programme, which also lists three works the volume doesn't print (Hewitt's *Polarity*, Williams's *Is There a Band?* and Thorpe's *Inrush*, entered below without notes) and, in each concert, a set by the Elder Conservatorium's Sonic Arts students (*Posthumanism 1* and *2*), not entered. The programme's special event, Keiichiro Shibuya's android opera *Scary Beauty* at the OzAsia Festival on Sunday 1 October at 2 pm, was not an ACMC presentation and is not entered.
