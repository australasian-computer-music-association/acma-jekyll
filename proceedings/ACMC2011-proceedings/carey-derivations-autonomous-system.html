_derivations: An Autonomous System for Responsive Live Music Improvisation

Benjamin Carey

In Proceedings of the Australasian Computer Music Conference 2011, University of Auckland, pp. 26–27. Australasian Computer Music Association, ISSN 1448-7780.

Abstract

Much recent research in the area of interactive computer music is concerned with the creation of computer systems that respond dynamically to musical input provided by improvising instrumentalists. These systems have the capacity to participate both responsively and autonomously in a live performance context, acting as virtual improvisers imbued with an awareness of the current and past musical context provided by one or more human improvisers. Various systems of this type strive for novelty and spontaneity in their responses, but also aim to coherently integrate computer-produced sounds with those of the musician. This research examines practical and aesthetic motivations behind systems that derive their sonic and algorithmic responses directly from an instrumentalist’s live performance. The current iteration of the author’s _derivations system is used as a case study to examine the design approach and contextualize this work in relation to earlier works by the author and relevant recent work of others in this field, particularly works that focus on timbre and sonority derived from the live instrument, rather than pattern or rule-based melodic invention.
