Stolen Goods (Stocketus)

Jos Mulder

In Proceedings of the Australasian Computer Music Conference 2018, Edith Cowan University, Perth, p. 123. Australasian Computer Music Association, ISSN 2206-5296.

Printed in the proceedings, p. 123 (PDF)

Format
acoustic ensemble and loudspeakers
Created
2017

Program note

Stolen Goods explores the immediate relation between acoustic instruments and loudspeakers projecting the sound of those instruments. A time delay is introduced between the two; at the start of the piece the loudspeakers wait 5 seconds before playing back the sound picked up (“stolen”) by the microphones on the instruments. This waiting time is brought down to nothing over the duration of the performance. Consequentially, different functions of the loudspeakers in relation to the instruments are activated, starting with an estranging echo effect becoming rhythmical musical elements, to ultimately becoming every-day sound projection (or amplification) when the delay time is reduced to zero. The metaphor of the pile of loudspeakers has its own character expressed by a soundscape of modulated and transformed recordings of earlier versions of the work.
