Mapping a 48-Note Just Intonation Scale to a Lumatone Keyboard

Amanda Cole

In ACMC 2024 Proceedings, The Australian Institute of Music, Sydney and Melbourne, Australia, p. 69. Australasian Computer Music Association.

Abstract

In this presentation I will share how I created my 48-note to the octave just intonation (JI) scale and how I have mapped it to the keys of my Lumatone keyboard. I will use diagrams to illustrate the construction of the scale and the keyboard layout. I will also show and explain my visual chord charts I use to perform my compositions in this tuning on my Lumatone. Just intonation intervals are found as the distance between harmonics in the harmonic series. My scale is a 7-limit tuning, meaning the largest prime factor contained in each of my interval ratios is seven. My 48 note scale is made from a 5-limit just intonation chromatic scale with twelve notes, which is transposed three times. I made my tuning by initially mapping it onto an Ervin Wilson lattice, where it is possible to see how each interval is multiplied by 1, 3, 5 and 7. My 48-note scale is made from a 5-limit just intonation chromatic scale with twelve notes, which is transposed three times. There are two 12- note chromatic scales which are 5-limit and these are transposed to create two 7-limit versions. To map my tuning to the Lumatone keyboard I adapted the Harmonic Table layout that comes as a preset. This layout is isomorphic meaning that chords can be transposed and played whilst keeping the same shape on the keyboard. I have arranged my notes in the Harmonic Table so that enharmonic notes are easily found and so that the 5-limit scales and 7-limit scales are next to each other. My keyboard mapping has four octaves of my scale. I have been enjoying composing and performing using this tuning and layout on my Lumatone keyboard.
