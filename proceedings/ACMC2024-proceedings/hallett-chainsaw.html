Creative work · Refereed

Chainsaw

Vicki Hallett

In ACMC 2024 Proceedings, The Australian Institute of Music, Sydney and Melbourne, Australia, pp. 73–74. Australasian Computer Music Association.

Program note

I am travelling with the Cofan people, one of the oldest surviving indigenous cultures in the Ecuadorian Amazon, to a remote jungle camp. The Cofans have lived in the area for centuries. It has taken us three days to reach this site. Sometimes, our only choice to get through the river, in its current low state, is by cutting the log or branch that has fallen across the river. Once cut (and then only enough to allow our passage), the tree and fallen logs remained within the rainforest system. Cutting is the last resort as it is dense timber and requires hot, heavy and dangerous work. Chainsawing while perched on the bow of a canoe, the aging chief of the Cofan takes on this job. Other options to gain access, (I counted 8 other options during our expedition) were chosen before resorting to the chainsaw, whether the log was dead or not. As the chainsaw wood dust drifts past, I reflect on the chopping down and mass destruction of the rainforest. The Cofan people have a very real and dangerous fight as they protect the forest. The reality of this fight is clear to our team. One of our guides had been kidnapped a few years ago and was scheduled to be executed. Luckily he escaped just before the execution. When we emerged from our camp a few days later, the reality was even more immediate to us, with the news of one of the Cofan leaders, Eduardo Mendua, being assassinated in his garden only 30 km or so away from where we were camping. Eduardo was assassinated because he was fighting to protect the forest from the horrors of mining extraction, timber harvesting and clearing for crops and livestock. Chainsaw is an introspective audio visual work about the Cofan people and other stewards and protectors of these forested areas within the Amazon Basin. We listen to the underwater sounds and the forest soundscape of one of the richest and most diverse ecosystems on our planet. Macaws caw above us. Turtles bask in the dappled sunlight with stunning blue Morpho butterflies on their backs. A true symbiosis. We have travelled through the meeting of two Rivers, home to the Pink Dolphins. Our utopian immersion is disrupted by a chainsaw, as it cuts through the dense rainforest timber. The chainsaw dust drifts past, on the surface of the water. It is a symbolic moment as we reflect on the imminent destruction of the jungle. A vital sequestration zone of the planet. The deforestation will continue until there is no more to take. Where does this leave the planet? Can it survive without the ecological balance? I am no longer thinking about our species’ survival - we have destroyed too much and passed the tipping point. But what about the other-than-humans? What will survive? How will it survive?
