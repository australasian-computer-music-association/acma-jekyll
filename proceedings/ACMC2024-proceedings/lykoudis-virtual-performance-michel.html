Creative work · Refereed

[Virtual] Performance of Michel Decoust's ‘Olos’

Pantelis Lykoudis

In ACMC 2024 Proceedings, The Australian Institute of Music, Sydney and Melbourne, Australia, pp. 13–14. Australasian Computer Music Association.

Program note

Michel Decoust studied at CNSMP under Jean Rivier and Darius Milhaud. The former’s ‘Grave et Presto’ and the latter’s ‘La Création du Monde’ constitute staples of the classical saxophone repertoire. However, composers from Decoust’s generation contributed to the instrument’s coming of age. The composer deconstructs the instrument his teachers wrote for with ‘Olos’ (1983), for tenor saxophone and electroacoustics, premiered by the great Daniel Kientzy. Decoust’s elegant diagram, outlining the 3 different means of filtering or prolonging the sound, reveals the influence of contemporary developments at IRCAM, where he served as director of education. Radu Stan’s introductory remarks provide an additional programmatic dimension to this underperformed gem: ‘In reverse the title gives solo. Otherwise with only a slight spelling change it gives aulos, a reed instrument commonly used by the ancient Greeks. The art of the aulos player consisted in giving the instrument its nomos, its field of expression. In the same spirit a present-day musician must acquire the field of play that is peculiar to the tenor saxophone. This is done in clearly defined stages. Types of attack, sound emission, rhythmic systems are differently conjugated each time creating new combinations. First the statement, through its easy rubato pace and ornamented formulae, attempts to borrow the air of a chant from far back in ancient tradition. The following plainsong is a ritornelle serving as an interlude to the multifarious events.’ The video presented in Australia will be recorded at studio AUX in Athens. My collaborator, audio engineer Giotis Paraskevaidis, is going to operate the synth, wahwah and delay the piece calls for, in addition to capturing all aspects of sound spatialization. Alternatively, I am open to travelling to Sydney and perform the piece live, with the assistance of a music technologist from the conference. However, I currently cannot afford the travel cost, which is why I am proposing the more realistic video recording. According to my recent experience in Seoul (International Computer Music Conference), video recordings with surround or ambisonic speaker setups were quite effective for the audience.
