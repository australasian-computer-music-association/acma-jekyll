Reverse-engineering Chaos: A Novel Approach to Electronic Music Production Inspired by Ed Rush & Optical’s 1998 Album ‘Wormhole’

Dylan F Sullivan

In ACMC 2024 Proceedings, The Australian Institute of Music, Sydney and Melbourne, Australia, pp. 44–45. Australasian Computer Music Association.

Abstract

This study uses an autoethnographic methodical design to understand and draw an indepth evaluation of the effectiveness of the methods and mental frameworks applied in the creation of the influential 1998 drum & bass album ‘Wormhole’. At the core of this research is a rigorous testing of the creative ideologies held by the duo responsible for the album – Ed Rush & Optical. Instead of directly analysing the original work, the duo’s unique ideas behind creativity and the music production process have been documented from various resources surrounding the time of the album’s creation. I have integrated these concepts into my own contemporary practice in the form of three music production experiments, facilitating the creation of three original works. These works were created under the requirements and limitations of an instructional model that I call ‘The Wormhole Method’. The resulting data indicates the potential value of this method to other contemporary electronic music producers as a tool to elicit unique creative solutions. ‘The Wormhole Method’ is a formula- based incubator for peculiar creative specimen(s). Its explicit effectiveness and ineffectiveness lie in the removal of conditions that participating creatives may take for granted in their usual practice, for better or for worse.
