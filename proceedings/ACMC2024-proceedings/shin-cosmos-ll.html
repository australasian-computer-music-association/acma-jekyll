Creative work · Refereed

Cosmos ll

Bokyung Shin

In ACMC 2024 Proceedings, The Australian Institute of Music, Sydney and Melbourne, Australia, pp. 20–21. Australasian Computer Music Association.

Program note

Cosmos II’ is a composition that reinterprets the moments of the birth of the universe, specifically the events that occurred during the first second after the Big Bang, from the perspective of the composer. The composition is divided into different sections, each representing time intervals ranging from the Planck epoch, the shortest unit of time with physical significance, to 10^(-35) seconds, 10^(-12) seconds, 10^(-6) seconds, and up to 1 second. Each section explores the yet-to-be-fully-understood physical laws and the four fundamental forces of the universe (gravity, electromagnetism, strong nuclear force, and weak nuclear force). It delves into the interactions among matter particles, antimatter particles, and photons, portraying the processes leading to the formation of the material world as we know it. To make these events perceptible in a musical form, the composer expands the events occurring within one second temporally and condenses them mathematically, expressing them through the language of music. Moreover, the composition incorporates radio noise as a musical material. This radio noise contains traces of cosmic microwave background radiation, evidence of the Big Bang cosmology. Through this incorporation, the composer enables listeners to feel the events that occurred approximately 13.8 billion years ago within the timeframe of our current existence.
