Invisible Territory: Sonifying the Game of Go

Mark Pedersen and Ralf Muhlberger

In ACMC05 Generate and Test: Proceedings of the Australasian Computer Music Conference 2005, Brisbane, Queensland University of Technology, pp. 116–122. Australasian Computer Music Association, ISSN 1448-7780.

Abstract

In this paper we explore issues of developing a sonification of the game of Go, and then adapting the sonification for use in an interactive “musification” featuring Shakuhachi, the Japanese end-blown flute, as a primary instrument for interaction and as a sound-source for the sonification. Our approach to sonification seeks to establish a mapping from data to sound which preserves the symbolism and the aesthetics of the game, as well as present the data embodied in a specific game record in a form that is as transparently comprehensible as possible. The final mode of presentation explored here takes a musical form incorporating live improvisation by a human performer, in order to promote an accessible and “humanised” result.
