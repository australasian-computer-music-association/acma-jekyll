Creative work

Resonances

Jim Barbour

In Proceedings of the Australasian Computer Music Conference 2002, Melbourne, RMIT University / Victorian College of Arts. Australasian Computer Music Association.

Program note

Resonances is in homage to Alvin Lucier, and takes his seminal work ‘I am sitting in a Room’ into a 21st century surround sound environment. Using a similar technique, Resonances explores the acoustic resonances in the three dimensional space of a performance venue, where the directionality of several voices are gradually overwhelmed by reflections and reverberation. Where once there were individual words with inherent meaning, in their place will be bell-like tones and melodies sharpened by occasional sibilance. By the end, all that will remain is a sound field without specific identity.

About the artist

Jim has extensive experience working with sound in Australia and overseas, with more than twenty years as a music producer and recording engineer and many years as a production manager in Commercial radio. Following on from Electrical Engineering studies at Adelaide University, Jim worked overseas and in Australia in the music industry producing audio for vinyl, CD, film, video and television releases. Credits include Recording Engineer for Men at Work’s ‘Business As Usual’, Sound Supervisor for the Australian Opera at the Adelaide Festival Theatre, film music awards for Aboriginal Film ‘Wrong Side of the Road’ and Advertising awards for radio production. Jim also worked at the Elder Conservatorium of Music and the Queensland Conservatorium of Music in their Electronic Music Studios, and tutored students in recording techniques and composition using electro-acoustic instruments and music synthesisers. He performed in concerts of experimental music and assisted in the creation of sound sculpture installations.

Over the last fourteen years, Jim has designed and delivered training programs at Swinburne University of Technology in Radio and Multimedia Authoring. He completed a Master of Arts in Communications at Swinburne in 2000, with a thesis titled ‘The Digitization of Acoustic Space: The Impact of new Digital Audio Technologies on Aural Perception’. Jim also holds a Graduate Diploma of Education in Information and Communications Technology from Melbourne University, specialising in Multimedia authoring and production.
