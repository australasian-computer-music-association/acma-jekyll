Creative work

The Secret of P.P.P.

Sibylle Pomorin

In Proceedings of the Australasian Computer Music Conference 2002, Melbourne, RMIT University / Victorian College of Arts. Australasian Computer Music Association.

Program note

An electro-acoustic composition - a commission for Netherlands Programme Service, 2000. Live-version for 8 loudspeakers arranged in a circle.

A major theme in my work since 1995 has been “The Voice” and, out of the voice, “Language” and it’s intellectual uses: communication, national musical languages, music as a means of self expression etc.

From the start of this period, I had also wanted to create an abstract electro-acoustic composition, which built on, out of and around the human voice. The commission from NPS-Radio provided the opportunity to realise that ambition.

I developed this piece with the team from NPS, who made the appropriate equipment available to me, and especially with the composer and sound engineer Arno Peeters, who worked with me on and off throughout this project.

To attune myself and to help the team to imagine their way towards their co-creative role, I provided a series of verbal, visual and acoustic evocations including: Gossamer, transparent, ethereal, floating, mysterious, steam, wreaths of myst, heavy snowflakes falling on a whitened landscape, rays of light, electricity, wind, flute-like melismas and glissandi, Tibetian bowls rubbed, the reverberation of a gong, the sound of a glasharmonica, high organ sounds and their overtones, flageolet, a shimmering murmur.

The singing of the Dutch soprano Jannie Pranger was to provide the building blocks out of which I intended to create the electro-acoustic arrangements. But, as I was working at the computer with the software offering me its vast variety of noises, I felt the need more and more to arrange the piece as musically as possible and to allow Jannie Pranger’s voice to shine through.

So although I held to some extend to the preconditions I had set, in the end the composition turned out rather different to how, at the start, I had imagined it would. But perhaps the beauty of the thing is just there - that even the composer is sometimes surprised by what emerges.

About the artist

Sibylle Pomorin (*1956) studied from 1975 until 1980 at the Staatliche Hochschule für Musik in Münster, specialising in flute and saxophone. At the same time she played in chamber-ensembles and Jazz-groups for which she also composed. She has toured, since 1983, with, among others, Peter Brötzmann, Joèlle Léandre, William Parker, Yosuke Yamashita, Chris Cutler, Conny Bauer. In 1984/85, she studied composition under Ernst Bechert in Hamburg. She attended further courses and classes under Dieter Schnebel, John Tchicai, Alice Shields and Eliane Radigue. She has initiated numerous ensembles and bands. Her music has been performed by herself and other interpreters throughout Europe and in Isreal, U.S.A, Argentinia and Australia. Since 1986, Sibylle Pomorin has lived as a freelance musician and composer in Berlin. She has received commissions for festivals, radio and theatre, various scholarships and awards as well as work/study visits to New York, Istanbul and Mexico. Lately she has received the first prize in the international competition „Soundscapes voor 2000” in Holland. Since 1994, she has worked in the electronic studios of the Columbia Princeton University in New York, the Center for Art and Media in Karlsruhe, the Technical University in Berlin and the Netherlands Programme Service in Hilversum. Most of her recent work has been chamber music, electro-acoustic music and works for radio. She has made seven records and CDs and she is featured in the film portrait „Neue Töne aus Berlin” by Carla Kilian (Bayerischer Rundfunk, March 1992).
