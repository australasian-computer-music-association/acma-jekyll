Creative work

True Nature

James Croson and Thawatpong Tangsajjapoj

In Proceedings of the Australasian Computer Music Conference 2002, Melbourne, RMIT University / Victorian College of Arts. Australasian Computer Music Association.

Program note

“True Nature” is an animation produced on computer. An artist living in the square-cornered/straight-lined world of the “golden section village” uses his drawing tools to design a cart and begins a journey. An accident leads him to a woman in a cottage in the woods who gives him a brush, with which he remakes his world with curves, enriching the village with a more organic nature.

The music was likewise realised on computer. Picking up on the golden section reference in the film, the music uses a scale based on ratios of phi. (The scene at the cottage and the fantasy sequence following are in equal temperament.) Many thanks to Norm Carey at the Eastman School of Music for discussion on the fancy scale. A few samples are used for sound effects, but all of the music employs instruments modelled in Csound and LORIS.

About the artists

Thawatpong Tangsajjapoj (also known as Pop) was engaged in architecture for eight years before turning to animation. He has produced three short films, and recently earned the MFA degree from the Rochester Institute of Technology, School of Film and Animation. Thawatpong lives in Bangkok, Thailand.

James Croson was born in El Paso, Texas. He has composed, performed, and recorded jazz in Columbus and Boston, and has degrees from the Eastman School of Music and the Ohio State University. His most recent efforts have been electro-acoustic music compositions and soundtracks for short films and animations. His “Aerial Displays” earned honorable mention at the Stockholm Electronic Arts Awards competition in 1998. Jim is pursuing a doctorate in composition at the Ohio State University, and lives in Mount Dora, Florida.
