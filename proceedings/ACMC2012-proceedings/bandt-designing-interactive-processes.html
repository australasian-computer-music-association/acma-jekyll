Artist talk

Designing Interactive Processes in Recent Environmental Sound Art: Blue Gold and Medusa Dreaming

Ros Bandt

In Interactive: Australasian Computer Music Conference 2012 Conference Proceedings, Brisbane, Queensland Conservatorium, Griffith University, pp. 99–103. Australasian Computer Music Association, ISSN 1448-7780.

Abstract

Interaction. Why and how do we want to relate to the environment? With whom and for whom? With what tools and media? What impact does our work have on the environment, its soundscape?

In two large scale environmental works, Blue Gold and Medusa Dreaming, processes of interaction will be identified, interrogating how acoustic space, sound, sculpture, film and creative musical interpretation of composed sound structures intersect. In analysing what is fixed and what is unfixed in the design process we can study different modalities of interaction and interdisciplinary collaboration at all stages of the creative process from the idea to audition. Human/environment/ human/machine, human/human interactive processes are endemic to sound art. These relationships will be identified in Blue Gold and Medusa Dreaming as they are embodied in aspects of the creative works: the site plan, the temporal and spatial design, sound recording, sound design, images, video, installation components, musical composition, to live performance and dispersion.
