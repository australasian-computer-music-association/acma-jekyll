Creative work

_derivations

Benjamin Carey

In Interactive: Australasian Computer Music Conference 2012 Conference Proceedings, Brisbane, Queensland Conservatorium, Griffith University. Australasian Computer Music Association, ISSN 1448-7780.

Program note

This composition presents an interactive performance system, designed to facilitate possible modes of interactivity between an instrumentalist and the computer in improvised performance. The system derives all of its sonic responses directly from the improvising instrumentalist; listening to, comparing and transforming analysed musical phrases stored in an ever expanding memory of past performer’s gestures. Part of the recent development of the _derivations software has been in facilitating the use of multiple ‘rehearsals’ sessions with the system in addition to information recorded and analysis during performance. The premise is that a rich database of cumulative interactions between human and machine will deepen and complicate any eventual live performance. For today’s performance, neither system nor performer will begin with a ‘blank memory’, as both system and recorder player will have been rehearsing for some time previously.

About the artist

Ben is a saxophonist/composer/technologist living in Sydney, and he is involved in the area of contemporary music performance of 20th and 21st century solo and chamber repertoire. He also works regularly with young composers in premiering new works for the saxophone in a variety of contexts. After his graduation from the Sydney Conservatorium in 2005, he spent two years in France studying saxophone the Conservatoire de Bordeaux. He is currently pursuing a PhD degree at the University of Technology, Sydney, where he is researching and developing systems for live interaction between instrumentalists and computer.
