OSynC: Cross-Application Performance Synchronisation of Beat-Driven Musics

Alex Mesker and Sarah Keith

In Interactive: Australasian Computer Music Conference 2012 Conference Proceedings, Brisbane, Queensland Conservatorium, Griffith University, pp. 51–56. Australasian Computer Music Association, ISSN 1448-7780.

Abstract

OSynC is a protocol consisting of standardised messages for communicating metrical information and rhythmic descriptors for use in networked computer-based music performance. It uses the Open Sound Control (OSC) encoding to send descriptors from a host machine to any number of receivers, while receivers can in turn send changes to the host. The OSC encoding is a de facto standard for musical control, and is an extensible, low-bandwidth method for communicating information independent of platform and application. OSynC places particular emphasis on communicating rhythmic musical information at a range of musical timescales, providing details on musical context that are desirable for performance of beat-driven musics. It also aims to provide flexibility for musical improvisation, as well as intelligibility for performers. The authors describe OSynC’s implementation as a Max patch, VST plug-in, and Max For Live device. These are available from http://x37v.com/x37v/osync/.
