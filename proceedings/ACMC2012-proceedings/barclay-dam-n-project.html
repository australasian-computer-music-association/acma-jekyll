Creative work

The DAM(N) Project: Jobat Calling

Leah Barclay and Jehan Kanga

In Interactive: Australasian Computer Music Conference 2012 Conference Proceedings, Brisbane, Queensland Conservatorium, Griffith University. Australasian Computer Music Association, ISSN 1448-7780.

Program note

Jobat Calling is a live and interactive audiovisual installation that connects Australian and Indian communities around a common concern: water security. The DAM(N) Project is a collaboration between director Jehan Kanga, vocal ensemble The Song Company, Bangalore based contemporary dance company Attakkalari, new/digital media company CuriousWorks and sound artist Leah Barclay. It presents the lives of remote communities in the Narmada Valley, displaced by large-scale dams developed to create power for Indian cities. The Project does not seek to engage in the heavily politicised issues around the Narmada, rather it tells stories taken from the many viewpoints and the living culture in the affected areas. The central idea within DAM(N) is the nature and mythology of water, its creative and destructive powers and our constant obsession to control it in the event of imminent scarcity. This is an issue that resonates with both the Indian and Australian psyche.

About the artists

Jehan is an emerging artistic director/producer - currently the assistant artistic director under Roland Peelman of The Song Company. His study as a baroque violin and as a director has taken him to Switzerland, Spain, Italy and USA. He has also directed several projects Australian Baroque Brass, The Pocket Score Company, Seven Teares, The Madrigal Society, Ensemble Con Brio and The Renaissance Players. He has produced and directed several collaborative productions – the most recent being Sweet Dreams with shadow puppeteer Stephen Mushin and The Song Company.

Leah Barclay is an award-winning composer and interdisciplinary artist recognised internationally for her distinctive sonic language. She creates immersive performance and multi-disciplinary installations at the intersection of art, science, technology and the environment. Leah’s recent major projects include Uravum (2011) with Attakkalari, Floating Land Festival as guest curator, Dhakan (ACMC11), Transvergence (2011) an installation on Cockatoo Island, Econtro Das Aguas commission for The Australian Voices, Mamori Sound Project in the Amazon, and lead artist within the Remnant/Emergency Artlab.
