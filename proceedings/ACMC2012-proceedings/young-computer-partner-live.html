Computer as Partner: Live Algorithms in Computational Systems

Michael Young

In Interactive: Australasian Computer Music Conference 2012 Conference Proceedings, Brisbane, Queensland Conservatorium, Griffith University, p. 1. Australasian Computer Music Association, ISSN 1448-7780.

Abstract

Computational systems able to collaborate with human improvisers are live algorithms: able to cooperate proactively, on an equal basis, with musicians in performance. This is an ideal that raises fundamental questions about creativity and group interaction, how these might be computationally modelled. Can musicians and computers relate to one another, just as human musicians do? Can an audience recognize and appraise this relationship? Live algorithms offer the prospect of a new understanding of real-time creative practice that differs from the established paradigms in live electronic music: computer as instrument and computer as proxy. Drawing upon ideas from social psychology and pragmatics, collective music-making can be viewed as a special case of social cooperation, evidenced primarily through sound. To attempt a functional description of live algorithms is to model modes of cooperation and causal attribution that occur between proactive agents in a shared sonic environment. The challenge of live algorithms is to find genuinely original ways for humans and computers to work together - an original way to make music. The ideal live algorithm paradigm is computer as partner.

About the author

Dr.Young is a composer with interests in real-time computer music, interdiscipinary practice and performance. He was born in South Shields in 1968. He studied music at Oxford University and composition with John Casken & Peter Manning at Durham University, completing a PhD in 1995. He is a Senior Lecturer in Music at Goldsmiths, University of London in the Electronic Music Studios (EMS). Previously, he lectured at Oxford Brookes University and University of Wales, Bangor. His recent music has focussed on interactive/generative systems, exploring the interface between composed and improvised music-making. He is co-founder of the Live Algorithms for Music Network. He has worked with jazz musicians and improvisers as pianist, laptop musician & composer. [source: http://homepage.mac.com/oobop/myounginfo/about.html]
