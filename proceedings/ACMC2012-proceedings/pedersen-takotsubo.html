Creative work

Takotsubo

Mark Pedersen and Roger Alsop

In Interactive: Australasian Computer Music Conference 2012 Conference Proceedings, Brisbane, Queensland Conservatorium, Griffith University. Australasian Computer Music Association, ISSN 1448-7780.

Program note

Takotsubo is an improvised Audio/Visual performance built around the interplay of three interfaces: traditional (guitar), the new (Kinect) and the ubiquitous (camera). These interfaces allow three different styles of interaction: the tactile and intended, the non-tactile and intended, and the non-tactile and unintended. This forms a tripartite interaction allowing varying and mutating degrees of agency and control for the participants (performers and audience).Each of these interactive systems affords different degrees of expertise and expression. In Takotsubo, the guitar, as traditional haptic music interface, provides fine and nuanced control to generate primary source audio material for the piece. Taking this material into a virtual spectral realm, it is then modified using live concatenative synthesis controlled by a Kinect-based gestural interaction system designed on principles of embodied music cognition. Finally, the audience, as participants in the performative moment, albeit without the same degree of agency as the performers, are included in the process via camera input into the generative audio system.

About the artists

Roger Alsop is a sound designer, composer, musician and media artist. His activities range through working with VicHealth, La Mama, and the Royal Botanic Gardens, musical and audiovisual improvisations, live mixing, and sound and media design for performance. He also teaches Sound Design (Victorian College of the Arts: UoM), Performative Architecture (Melbourne School of Design; UoM), and Production Styles at Box Hill Institute. His interests are in developing interactive approaches for developing creative works that enhance the fundamentally hybrid nature of modern creativity.

Mark Pedersen is a Melbourne-based artist working primarily in sound and image design, and is currently doing a research Masters on gestural control systems for computer music performance at the Victorian College of the Arts: UoM. He currently co-hosts the Melbourne SeenSound: Visual/Music series with Brigid Bourke, has recorded and performed in India and Senegal with the Indian-Australian trio Hathira Sama, and his collaborative work with Melbourne poet and novelist Melike Ülgezer was recently published online in Cordite Poetry Review. His primary interests lie in creating environments for expressive performance which explore the connection between the physical and the virtual. Examples of his work can be found at www.chailight.com.
