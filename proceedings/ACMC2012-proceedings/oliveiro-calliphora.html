Creative work

Calliphora

Mark Oliveiro

In Interactive: Australasian Computer Music Conference 2012 Conference Proceedings, Brisbane, Queensland Conservatorium, Griffith University. Australasian Computer Music Association, ISSN 1448-7780.

Program note

Calliphora is a beautifully misleading word which refers, not to an idyllic fantasy island set on crystal blue waters, but the genus of the common Blowfly. Using the numerology of the Flies life cycle and computer generated sounds which aim to mimic the sonifications of these insectoid organisms, Calliphora is a segmented work of alien sounds in a familiar environment. As if under the microscope, studied from various angles, this work attempts to find beauty and interest in a subject most find abhorrent. Calliphora was written for another striking and misunderstood phenomenon, the bass recorder. Flies are still gross, though.

About the artist

Born in Sydney, Australia in 1983. His music has been performed by the Darwin Symphony Orchestra and Orchestra Victoria, by the Sydney Eclectic Composer Society, BIT20, The Juventas Ensemble, Nexas Sax, the Song Company, Pictures on Silence, and Chronology Arts, and international appearances at the workshop for Young Composers in Latvia (2006) and the International Festival of Stresa, Italy (2006). He has studied at the University of Sydney (Australia), Indiana University (USA) and is currently in pursuing a PhD degree at the University of North Texas. His teachers include Michael Smetanin, Mary Finsterer, Luca rancesconi, Klas Torstensson, John Gibson, Don Freund, Andew May, Joseph Klein and Jon Nelson.
