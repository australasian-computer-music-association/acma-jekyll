Creative work

Hibiki 1.0

Guillaume Potard, Stephen Barrass, and Olivier Zeller

In Interactive: Australasian Computer Music Conference 2012 Conference Proceedings, Brisbane, Queensland Conservatorium, Griffith University. Australasian Computer Music Association, ISSN 1448-7780.

Program note

Hibiki 1.0 is part an interactive sound installation and part a musical piece. Hibiki 1.0 will implement real-time sonification of data streams obtained from the internet. Such data may be collected using specialised online tools such as Nimbits which makes available hundreds of sensor data streams. The focus of Hibiki is to sonify environmental data and the impact of human consumption of resources; this represents a sonification layer. Other sonification layers may also be implemented, for example: Internet traffic, the weather, stock exchange data etc.

Another important aspect of Hibiki is interactivity: using their web-enabled mobile phones, the public will be able to modify parameters of the sonification mapping and the current sonification layer. We implement interactivity using an HTML5 web page and custom server-side software which makes our technique cross-platform and no native app installation is required. Hibiki will be implemented as a permanent sound installation in the Nishi building which is a modern and 6 green star rated building complex currently being developed in Canberra. Hibiki was commissioned as an experiment in architectural audio, data sonification and user interactivity.

Hibiki will be shown for the first time to the public at ACMC 2012.

About the artists

Guillaume Potard is a Sydney-based sound researcher and artist. In 2006 he obtained a PhD on ‘3-D audio object oriented coding’ from the University of Wollongong. Some of his research interests are: acoustics, digital signal processing, perception of sound (psychoacoustics), design of novel interactive multimedia systems, data sonification and installation art. After working 6 years at Dolby Labs in Sydney, Guillaume started Lofty Consulting in 2011, a consultancy firm which provides R&D and engineering services in Digital Signal Processing, sound and multimedia systems. Guillaume is the main author on two patents.

Stephen Barrass is an academic, researcher and artist with interests in data sonification, sound art, digital design and fabrication, interactive storytelling, social media in creative collaboration, physical computing, immersive and wearable interfaces. He holds a Ph.D. in Information Technology from the Australian National University on the topic of Auditory Information Design (1998) and a Bachelor of Electrical Engineering from the University of NSW with an honours dissertation on Digital Holograms (1987). His creative works include the Welcome Space at the National Museum of Australia 2001~, OpShop at Experimenta 2003, Listening to the Mind Listening at the Sydney Opera House Studio 2004, Baroque EEG at Sound and Musci Computing in Barcelona 2010, and Zizi the Affectionate Couch at MONA 2011~.

Olivier Zeller was born and raised in Paris. He has lived and worked in Paris, London and Sydney over the last 15 years. As an avid student of music and all things technology, he has completed higher education in a range of disciplines, including a diploma in sound engineering at SAE Paris, a Master in computer science at Paris V and INSA Toulouse, as well as studies in music composition at the Australian Institute of Music (Sydney). His professional life has been just as varied, ranging from software developer for IRCAM in Paris and a DSP design engineer for Dolby in Sydney. In 2008 he became an independent music producer and DJ, specialising in electronic music, with a focus on dubstep.
