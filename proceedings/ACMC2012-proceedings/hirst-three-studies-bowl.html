Creative work

Three Studies for Bowl and Computer

David Hirst

In Interactive: Australasian Computer Music Conference 2012 Conference Proceedings, Brisbane, Queensland Conservatorium, Griffith University. Australasian Computer Music Association, ISSN 1448-7780.

Program note

Three Studies for Bowl and Computer uses a number of sounds from a set of stainless steel kitchen bowls, and several thunderstorms that I recorded at the beachside town of Torquay where we holiday every summer. In Study 101: Thunderbowl, I used the basic two classes of sound, the bowl and the thunderstorm, literally at first, then they begin to interact and influence each other as the piece progresses. Then the thunderstorm raindrops are actually playing the bowl. Finally, the thunderstorm and the bowl sounds separate and we hear them in their natural form at the end. Study 102: Scrabbling uses scraping bowl sounds with movement and processing. Study 103: Reson is probably the most ‘electronic’ sounding of the three studies, but it still uses the bowls as the source sounds.

About the artist

Hirst has lectured on electronic music at the Tasmanian Conservatorium of Music and La Trobe University, where he served as Chairperson of the Music Department from 1993 to 1995. In 1997, Hirst studied composition with Jonty Harrison at the University of Birmingham. He has published extensively on electroacoustic music, in journals such as Organised Sound, Array, Chroma, Sounds Australian, New Music Articles, Press Press Magazine, and he was the founding editor of Mikropolyphonie, the online contemporary music journal. Hirst was a Senior Lecturer in Educational Design at the University of Melbourne from 2000 to 2007. At the University of Melbourne, he completed a PhD with a music composition folio and a music psychology thesis entitled The Development of a Cognitive Framework for the Analysis of Acousmatic Music, and subsequently became a Senior Research Fellow in the Music and Neurocognition Laboratory of the School of Behavioural Sciences. Since 2008, he has been Senior Lecturer in Curriculum and Academic Development at La Trobe University. Hirst’s works have been released by Frog Peak Music, USA, PeP Recordings, Canada, the Australian Computer Music Association, and NMA Tapes, Australia
