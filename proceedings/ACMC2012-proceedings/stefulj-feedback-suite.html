Creative work

Feedback Suite

John Stefulj and Kim Cunio

In Interactive: Australasian Computer Music Conference 2012 Conference Proceedings, Brisbane, Queensland Conservatorium, Griffith University. Australasian Computer Music Association, ISSN 1448-7780.

Program note

In several brief sessions (recorded at the Queensland Conservatorium’s IMERSD studio), Stefulj free improvised, making sonic beds with a suite of wind and percussion, then handed these artifacts to Cunio for re-composition, processing and manipulation. In some sessions, Cunio suggested motifs or fragments to play, which Stefulj interpreted in real time. These fragments were then taken away and manipulated extensively. Finally the artists returned to the IMERSD studio to listen to and finalise the music, taking stock of underlying aesthetics and the flow of the project as a whole.

In this conference performance, there are four stereo pairs of processed audio playing alongside the live performance. The performance is primarily a re-imagination of the original project, utilising many of the devices of the recording in real time, with the option of radical changes in the improvised field depending on the nature of the performance. In some instances, harmonic creations (made from time-stretched single drone notes) are left intact for counter-melody to resurface as a live component.

About the artists

Dr Kim Cunio has studied with a number of Australia’s finest musicians including Australian composer Nigel Butterly, conductor and producer Eric Clapham, and Jazz guitar legend Ike Isaacs. His work with the ABC has seen him compose and produce music projects for CD, radio and television over the last decade. He is one of Australia’s most accomplished researching composers and was awarded an ABC Golden Manuscript Award in 2004 in recognition of his work with traditional and Islamic music.

Kim works in new art music, music research, traditional music, acousmatics and screen. He plays a large number of traditional instruments and his music is expansive, beautiful and multi layered. He has a state of the art studio and lectures at the Queensland Conservatorium.

John Stefulj has composed, arranged, performed, recorded, researched and taught with artists throughout Australia as a professional multi-instrumentalist and educator since the 1980s. His work continues within and across a variety of contemporary, traditional and indigenous music as part of his lifelong commitment to cultural enhancement throughout communities, including activities in ensembles, recordings, clinics and festivals. Through primary, secondary and tertiary level teaching, John has shared his experiences from a broad range of music and associated environments, and similarly, he finds teaching to be integral to his sense of creativity and contribution in performance. John realises his enthusiasm towards composed and improvised music by blending both approaches comfortably, which has led him to experiment in many acoustic and electronic mediums, permitting him the enjoyment of a broadening media base through involvement in a range of educational, theatre, film, television, radio, street performance, multimedia environments and other art forms.
